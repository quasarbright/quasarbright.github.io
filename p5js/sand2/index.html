Sand

The box is a grid of 100 × 100 cells, and each cell holds at most one grain. Sixty times a second, every grain gets one turn to move or react. A grain only looks at the 8 cells touching it, and what it does depends on its material. Everything you see, from heaps to puddles to spreading fires, comes from those local rules.

Moving

Sand and gunpowder fall straight down if they can, otherwise slide diagonally down, which is what makes slopes. They sink through liquids by swapping places with them. Liquids try the same, then slide sideways, which is what flattens a puddle. Lava is thick: most turns it doesn't spread. Two liquids side by side occasionally swap, so they slowly mix. Stone and wood never move.

Turns go from the bottom row up, so a falling grain lands in the space the grain under it just left, and a column of sand falls together instead of one row per turn.

Reacting

Each turn, a grain gets a chance to act on each neighbor:

  • Acid dissolves wood (10%), sand (5%) and stone (1%).
  • Fire lights gasoline and gunpowder (10%) and wood (1%). It burns out over time, faster with empty space or non-flammable things around it, and water puts it out at once.
  • Lava lights flammable things the same way and melts stone into more lava (0.3%). It cools over time and turns to stone; touching water cools it instantly.
