Rock Paper Scissors

Fill a grid with rock, paper and scissors at random, and let every square lose to its neighbors by the usual rules. Rock gets covered by paper, paper gets cut by scissors, scissors get smashed by rock. Nobody wins for good, so the colors chase each other around forever, and the chase winds itself into spinning spirals.

the rule

Each square of the grid (a cell) holds one species. With more than three species they sit in a circle: each one is beaten by the next and wraps around at the end. Every step, every cell counts how many of its neighbors are the species that beats it. If that count reaches the threshold, the cell is taken over and becomes that species. (With chance below 100%, it only might be.) The neighbors are the cells within reach, in a square, diamond or circle.

why spirals

Look for a point where, walking a small loop around it, you pass every species in order. Each species keeps eating the one behind it, so the boundaries rotate around that point forever and wind up into a spiral. A random start has thousands of these points. They crowd each other out until a few big spirals own the screen.

The threshold is what makes them clean. With a threshold of 1 a single enemy is enough, and almost every random cell has one, so the whole grid just flips in lockstep and stays static noise. Raise the threshold and lone specks can't take anything over. Only solid fronts advance, the sharp tips of a front stall because they touch few cells, and the fronts round off and curl.

The neighborhood's shape becomes the waves' shape: a square neighborhood grows waves with corners, a circle grows round ones. Each preset picks a neighborhood and then, for each species count, the threshold that grows the best spirals. Too low and the field flips in lockstep as static; too high and nothing can be taken over at all. More species put more bands of color in every arm.

play with it

Move the mouse (or drag a finger) over the field to spray fresh randomness, which seeds new spirals.
