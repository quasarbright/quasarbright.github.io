Rock Paper Scissors

Fill a grid with rock, paper and scissors at random, and let each square be taken over by its neighbors, by the usual rules. Nobody wins for good, so the colors chase each other around forever, and the chase winds itself into spinning spirals.

who beats whom

Each square of the grid (a cell) holds one species. With three species it's the game you know: rock smashes scissors, scissors cut paper, paper covers rock. With more, the species sit around a circle, and each one beats exactly one other: the next one clockwise. Every species beats one and loses to one. Species that aren't next to each other on the circle ignore each other, so a cell can only ever be taken over by the one species that beats it.

the rule

Every step, every cell counts how many of its neighbors are the species that beats it. If at least a threshold of them are, the cell is taken over and becomes that species. Here a cell looks at its 8 touching neighbors, and 3 of them have to beat it.

why spirals

Find a point where, walking a small loop around it, you pass every species in order. Each species keeps taking over the one behind it, so the boundaries around that point rotate forever and wind up into a spiral. The dots below mark every such point, computed live. A random start has thousands of them; they crowd each other out until a few big spirals own the space.

the threshold

The threshold is what decides whether spirals appear at all. A random cell has about 8 ÷ 3 ≈ 2.7 neighbors that beat it. Set the threshold below that and nearly every cell gets taken over every step: the whole grid flips together and stays static (the first grid below is slowed down so you can see it). Set it well above and almost nothing can be taken over, so the noise freezes. Just above the average, lone specks can't win but solid fronts can, and the fronts curl into spirals.

the first version

The first version of this page used a threshold of 1. From random noise that only flickers, so it started from smooth blobs instead. With one enemy enough, a front moves one cell in every direction each step, and "one cell in every direction" on the 8 touching cells is a square. That's why it grew squares, and it's the original rule here.

neighborhood shape

The shape of the area a cell looks at becomes the shape of the waves. Below, each grid starts as one species with a tiny pinwheel of all three in the middle, and a single enemy is enough. Each step a front moves to every cell its neighborhood reaches, so a square neighborhood grows a square spiral, a diamond grows a diamond, and a circle grows a round one.

play with it

Move the mouse (or drag a finger) over the field to spray fresh randomness and seed new spirals. More species put more bands of color in every arm. The soft rule makes each takeover a coin flip, which wobbles the edges.
